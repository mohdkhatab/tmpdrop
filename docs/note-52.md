# Note 52: Sending a tmpdrop link to a group or team

A tmpdrop link is easy to forward, so a link meant for a small group can reach people you never intended. A little planning keeps the sharing tidy.

## One link, one purpose

- Send one link for one set of files. Mixing several unrelated files into one link makes it harder for people to find what they need, and harder for you to know what you shared.
- If different people need different files, send separate links. It is a small extra step, and it avoids giving someone access to files that are not theirs.

## Who needs it?

- Before sending, list who actually needs the files. A group chat with twenty people is not the same as the three people working on the task.
- If you share in a channel or group, say who the files are for in the message itself: "For the design review group" is clearer than a bare link.
- Avoid posting a tmpdrop link in a public channel or forum unless the files are genuinely public. Once a link is posted publicly, you cannot control where it goes next.

## The message around the link

- Name the files, say what recipients should do with them, and give the expiry date. For example: "Here are the slides for Friday's review. Please download them before Monday, when the link expires."
- Ask one person to confirm they received the files, rather than waiting for the whole group to reply.

## After the group has the files

- Once the work is done, you do not need to do anything special: the link will expire on its own. If you sent it by mistake to the wrong group, tell the group not to download or forward it, and send a fresh link to the right people.
- If the files change, send a new link rather than editing the message. An updated link with a clear message avoids the confusion of two versions floating around the group.
